# Concordant English rationale - audit log

Audit log of the translator. Contains those entries whose concordant English choice required an explicit weighing between multiple linguistically defensible candidates, including the reasoning that led to the final choice. This is not an approval list; every choice has been made. The document serves as a reference for verification of the reasoning path.

Source-discipline: this rationale derives entries solely from seven-anchor linguistic analysis. Existing English translation traditions (KJV, NIV, ESV, NASB, RSV, NRSV, NET, AMP, MEV, LSB, etc.) are admissible only as external reference and are explicitly named when relevant. They are never the authority for the concordant choice.

Methodology (constant for all batches): per Strong the concordant choice is made on seven linguistic anchors - root, morphology, stem-family, cognates, Septuagint as linguistic witness, word-formation, and distribution across the entire Scripture (all occurrences contextually analysed to find the common denominator of meaning). No tradition, no theology, no school-preference. The translator decides.

## Hebrew

### Batch 1 (Hc-H94, 2026-04-29)

#### Prefix particles (Hc, Hd, Hl, Hb, Hm, Hk, Hi, Hs)

**Hc waw -> and**
The proclitic conjunction is overwhelmingly additive. KJV/ESV/NIV use 'and' as default with context-shifts to 'but/then/now/even'. The seven-anchor distribution analysis confirms that the additive function is the common denominator across all 51,170 occurrences; adversative and consecutive uses are syntactic shadings of the same basic connector. 'And' is the concordant minimum.

**Hd ha -> the**
English 'the' has no gender or number inflection, which simplifies the rendering relative to Dutch (where 'de/het' splits on grammatical gender). The concordant choice is uniform.

**Hl le -> to**
Considered: to, for, unto, towards. The Hebrew prefix expresses dative and direction. 'For' carries a benefactive sense that drifts into purpose; 'unto' is archaic and English-Bible-traditional but not concordantly tighter than 'to'. KJV uses 'unto' frequently; ESV/NIV mostly use 'to/for'. 'To' preserves the dative-directional core most economically without leaning on tradition. Context-splinters 'for' and 'towards' surface in render but are derivable from the kernel.

**Hb be -> in**
Locative-instrumental. English 'in' covers the static spatial sense; instrumental 'with/by' surfaces in render contexts but is not the kernel.

**Hm mi -> from**
Considered: from, of, out-of. The Hebrew prefix is a shortened form of min (H4480a, ablative). 'From' captures the ablative-directional kernel. 'Of' surfaces in partitive contexts but is a downstream development. 'Out of' is the natural rendering of full-form min, not the prefix variant.

**Hk ke -> as**
Comparative kernel. 'Like' and 'as' are near-synonyms in English; 'like' carries the prepositional-noun sense ('like a lion') while 'as' carries the conjunctive comparative sense ('as a lion roars'). Hebrew prefix ke covers both. 'As' is chosen as the more grammatically flexible base form; 'like' would clash with the independent lexeme H3644 kemo / H834d ka-asher 'like'.

**Hi ha (interrogative) -> ?**
English has no prefixal interrogative particle. The concordant choice mirrors the NL approach: render as the bare question mark in output, since translating it as 'is it that' or 'whether' would inject syntax not present in the source.

**Hs she -> which**
Late-Hebrew proclitic relative, sister to asher (H834a). English has 'which/that/who' for the relative; 'which' is chosen as the most generic form that does not preselect for animacy. 'That' or 'who' surface in render contexts.

#### Avad-family (H6-H13)

The avad-family is one of the major destruction-and-loss complexes in Hebrew. The concordant English chain mirrors the NL approach: each derivative form keeps a hyphenated compound that surfaces both the loss-aspect and the destruction-aspect.

**H6 avad -> perish-be-lost**
Considered: perish, be lost, vanish, destroy, be undone. The verb covers both 'a sheep is lost' (Lev 5:23) and 'a person perishes' (Num 24:20). KJV/ESV split the renderings by context, which destroys the concordance. The hyphenated 'perish-be-lost' preserves both poles of the kernel: cessation of presence (lost) and cessation of being (perish). External reference NRSV 'perish' alone leaves the lost-object reading underweight.

**H8 oved -> perishing-lost-going-down**
Active participle of H6. Used twice (Num 24:20,24) for nations 'going down' to destruction. The triple-hyphenation captures the durative aspect (perishing as ongoing process) rather than the punctiliar 'has perished'. NL pattern followed.

**H10 avaddoh -> destruction-ruin-extermination**
Abstract noun from the same root. Used in Prov 27:20 paired with Sheol, and Num 24:20 for the 'end' of Amalek. The triple-hyphenation distinguishes from H11 avaddon (place-name) and H12 avdan (state-noun), and preserves the abstract-cumulative sense.

**H11 avaddon -> perdition-destruction-place-Abaddon**
The toponymic and conceptual senses cluster: place of perdition alongside Sheol, occasionally personified. NT external reference: Rev 9:11 transliterates as Abaddon (paired with Greek Apollyon). The hyphenated rendering preserves both the noun-of-state ('perdition') and the proper-noun-of-place ('Abaddon'). KJV uses 'destruction' alone, which loses the toponym; NIV uses 'Abaddon' alone, which loses the conceptual layer.

#### Avah-family (H14, H34)

**H14 avah -> be-willing**
Considered: be-willing, consent, want, desire. The verb is overwhelmingly used in negation ('they would not'). The kernel is volitional consent rather than appetitive desire (which is H7521 ratsah, H2654 chafets). 'Be-willing' captures the modal-volitional core. 'Want' would conflate with appetitive vocabulary.

**H34 evyon -> poor-needy**
Considered: poor, needy, destitute, beggar. The noun derives from avah (long-for, desire) and refers to the social class characterised by lack and longing. Hebrew has multiple poverty-words (ani, dal, evyon, rash, miskeen). The concordant pair 'poor-needy' captures both the material lack (poor) and the longing-for-help (needy from av-root). NL pattern followed.

#### Abir-family (H46, H47)

**H46 abir -> bull-mighty**
Used six times, all of them as titles or metaphors involving the divine ('Mighty One of Jacob', Gen 49:24) or leader-stallions. The semantic range covers the literal bull and the figurative mighty one. KJV uses 'mighty' alone, which loses the bull-image. The hyphenated form preserves both poles. Distinguished from H47 abbir (the adjective form).

**H47 abbir -> strong-mighty**
Adjective form. Used 17 times for warriors, prey-birds, bulls. The hyphenation marks intensity. Distinguished from H46 by morphology (adjective vs noun-title) and by the loss of the explicit bull-image in the adjective.

#### Aval-family (H56-H61)

**H56 aval -> mourn**
Verb. Standard semantic field; the kernel is grief-expression for the dead or for calamity.

**H57 avel -> mourning** (adjectival)
**H60 evel -> mourning** (substantival)

The two related forms share a single English rendering 'mourning' but differ morphologically. Because English has only one word for the noun and the participle, the concordant choice is forced to use it for both, with the woordsoort field carrying the disambiguation. NL preserves the distinction with 'rouwend' (participle) vs 'rouw' (noun).

**H61 aval -> verily**
Homonym of H56 (same consonants, different root). Affirmative/adversative particle. Considered: verily, truly, indeed, however, but. KJV uses 'verily/but' depending on context. 'Verily' is chosen as the affirmative kernel; the adversative use is a contextual shading.

#### Stone-family (H68-H72)

**H68 even -> stone**
Standard. Unique lexeme.

**H70 ovnayim -> birth-stones-potter's-wheel**
Dual form of H68 covering two distinct objects: the birthing-stones (Ex 1:16, the chair on which Hebrew women gave birth) and the potter's wheel (Jer 18:3). The concordant rendering preserves both, using the dual-form indication that they are paired-stones objects.

**H72 Even-ha-Ezer -> Ebenezer**
Compound proper noun. English convention 'Ebenezer' (from KJV/RSV onward) merges the construct phrase. The literal sense 'stone-of-help' is preserved in the toelichting.

#### Avraham/Avram (H85, H87)

**H85 Avraham -> Abraham**
Standard English convention. The narrative-name change in Gen 17:5 is preserved by keeping H85 and H87 as separate Strong-codes.

**H87 avram -> Abram**
Pre-covenant name. Concordantly transliterated.

### Batch 2 (H95-H192, 2026-04-29)

#### Adon-family (H113, H136)

**H113 adon -> lord**
Considered: lord, master, sovereign, sir. The lexeme covers all of these in distribution. KJV/ESV/NIV split renderings by context: "lord" for divine, "master" for human master, "sir" for polite address. The seven-anchor distribution analysis confirms that the kernel is a hierarchical authority-relation; the divine-vs-human split is contextual. 'Lord' (lowercase) holds the authority-kernel and covers all 326 occurrences. Capitalisation is a rendering choice (when the referent is YHWH or the Christ), not a lexical distinction.

**H136 Adonay -> my-Lord**
Morphologically a 1cs-pronominal form of adon. Hyphenation preserves the composition. Capitalised because the form is reserved as a divine address in the MT (alongside YHWH); KJV uses "Lord" (capitalised). The morpheme-by-morpheme reading "my-lord(s)" is pluralis-honorificus with first-person suffix.

#### Adam-family (H119, H120, H121, H122a, H122b, H125, H132)

**H120 adam -> human** (significant choice)
Considered: man, mankind, humanity, human, person. KJV/ESV/NIV use 'man' as default. The seven-anchor analysis: the Hebrew adam is grammatically masculine but semantically inclusive (Gen 1:27 'male and female he created them' both within ha-adam; Gen 5:2 'male and female he created them, and called their name adam'). Modern English 'man' is gender-loaded and excludes the female reference. 'Human' preserves the species-meaning without the male bias. NL chose 'mens' (gender-neutral). External-reference NRSV uses 'humankind/humanity' for the same reason. 'Human' is the concordant minimum that holds both individual and collective senses without inserting gender that the source does not specify. Note: when the form 'ben-adam' (son of man) appears, the rendering becomes 'son-of-human' which is concordant but unusual; this is preferred over breaking the lexical concordance.

**H121 Adam-I -> Adam** (proper noun)
Kept as proper noun where the context unambiguously refers to the first-human as a named individual (Gen 4:25, 5:1-5 etc). The homograph with H120 adam is preserved by the Strong-code distinction.

**H122a adom -> red** vs **H122b adom-paard -> sorrel-russet-horse-colour**
Same lexeme split by Zechariah's distinct horse-colour usage. H122a covers the general red; H122b is specifically the horse-coat-colour 'sorrel/russet'. NL pattern followed.

#### Iggera/Iggeret (H104, H107)

**H107 iggeret -> letter**
Standard. Distinguished from H5612 sefer 'book/scroll' (broader category) and H4385 mikhtav 'writing'.

**H104 iggera (Aramaic) -> missive-letter-aramaic**
Aramaic cognate of H107. Hyphenated with '-aramaic' suffix (as for H2 av-aramaic, H4 ev-aramaic) to mark the Aramaic provenance.

#### Ahav-family (H157, H158, H159, H160)

**H157 ahav (verb) -> love**, **H160 ahavah (noun) -> love**
The English language collapses the verb and the noun under the same word 'love'. The distinction is preserved through the woordsoort field (verb vs noun) rather than through different lexemes. NL has the same collapse ('liefhebben' verb, 'liefde' noun, but the kernel is the same). External-reference: KJV 'love' for both. No alternative would preserve the concordance better.

**H158 ahavim -> love-caresses-plural**
Plural form, used twice in poetic context (Prov 5:19, Hos 8:9). Hyphenated to mark the plural-collective sense distinct from the abstract H160 ahavah.

#### Avah-homonym pair (H14, H183)

H14 avah 'be-willing' and H183 avah 'crave' are written identically but are separate roots with distinct semantic fields. H14 is volitional (consent, agree); H183 is appetitive (desire, crave intensely). The English distinction holds: 'be-willing' for H14, 'crave' for H183.

#### Interjection-of-distress (H17, H162, H188, H190)

**H17 abui -> alas!** (lowercase)
**H162 ahah -> Alas!** (capitalised)
The two share the lament-cry function but H17 is hapax and brief, H162 is more emphatic and frequently directed in address ('Alas, Lord YHWH!', Jer 1:6). The capitalisation distinguishes register in rendering. Both are onomatopoeic.

**H188 oy -> woe-cry**
**H190 oyah -> woe-is-me**
Standard biblical Hebrew lament-cries; the composition 'woe-is-me' for H190 reflects the emphatic personal form.

#### Fool (H191)

**H191 ewil -> fool**
Hebrew has multiple fool-words: ewil, kesil (H3684), sakhal (H5530), naval (H5036). Each carries a distinct nuance. ewil is the morally coarse, unbending fool. The single English 'fool' is unavoidable; the woordsoort field and toelichting carry the disambiguation. KJV uses 'fool/foolish' for all of them, which destroys the concordance. We preserve the distinction by keeping each H-code's toelichting clear about which fool-type is meant.

### Batch 3 (H193a-H285, 2026-04-29)

#### Aven-family (H205, H206, H209)

**H205 aven -> iniquity**
Considered: iniquity, vanity, wickedness, mischief, trouble. The Hebrew aven covers moral wrong, calamity, emptiness. KJV is inconsistent (iniquity/vanity/wickedness depending on context). The kernel is moral evil with an undertone of emptiness. 'Iniquity' preserves the moral weight; 'vanity' (which KJV uses in some places) loses the wrongness; 'wickedness' clashes with H7562 resha. The seven-anchor distribution analysis confirms 'iniquity' as the dominant moral kernel across all 80 occurrences.

**H206 aven-II-plaats -> Aven (place)**
Same consonants as H205 but as a toponym for Heliopolis (Egyptian On). Wordplay: the prophets exploit the homography between the place-name and the moral term. Concordant rendering preserves both: 'proper-name-Aven-Heliopolis-Egyptian-On'.

#### Or-light-family (H215, H216, H217a, H217b, H219a)

**H216 or (noun) -> light**
Standard. Unique lexeme.

**H215 or (verb) -> give-light**
The hyphenated 'give-light' marks the causative aspect (Hiphil predominant) over the simple Qal 'shine'. Distinguishes verb from noun in render.

**H217a ur-I -> light-fire-flash**
Hapax-tendency. The compound preserves the dual sense of luminous-fire (judgment, oracle) and the flash-aspect that distinguishes from H216 or 'light' and H784 esh 'fire'. NL pattern followed.

#### Ezrach-native-born (H249)

**H249 ezrach -> native-born**
Considered: native, native-born, citizen, indigenous. The Hebrew is a substantive 'one risen up [in the land]', from zarach (rise). KJV/ESV use 'native-born' or 'home-born'. 'Native-born' captures the distinction from ger (sojourner, H1616) without loading modern citizenship-concepts.

#### Brother-sister pair (H251, H269)

**H251 ach -> brother**
**H269 achot -> sister**
Standard. Unlike H120 adam (which is gender-neutral species-noun), ach and achot are explicitly gendered kinship-terms. Hebrew has no separate generic 'sibling' word. Source-discipline holds: the gender is in the source.

#### Achaz-family (H270, H271, H272, H274)

**H270 achaz (verb) -> seize-grasp**
Considered: seize, grasp, hold, take-hold-of, fasten. The hyphenated 'seize-grasp' preserves both the punctiliar (seize) and the durative (grasp/hold) aspects. KJV varies between 'take/hold/lay-hold-of'. Concordant 'seize-grasp' covers the kernel.

**H272 achuzah -> possession**
Derived from achaz; what is grasped/held. Predominantly land-possession. 'Possession' is the standard equivalent.

**H271 Achaz / H274 Achazyahu -> Ahaz / Ahaziah**
Proper-noun forms of the same root. Standard English biblical convention.

#### Echad-numeral (H259)

**H259 echad -> one**
Considered: one, a/an (indefinite article use). Hebrew echad serves as both cardinal numeral and (less commonly) as indefinite article. English separates these into 'one' (numeral) and 'a/an' (article). The concordant choice 'one' holds the cardinal kernel; the indefinite-article surface is a render-context shading. NL 'een' covers both functions naturally; English forces the choice.

### Batch 4 (H286-H576b, 2026-04-29) — 300 entries

#### El-Elohim-Eloah triad (H410, H430, H433, H426)

**H410 El -> Mighty-One**
**H430 elohim -> Mighty-One**
**H433 eloah -> Mighty-One**
**H426 Elah-Aramaic -> Mighty-One**

This is the most consequential batch-4 decision for English readers. The dominant English biblical tradition (KJV, NIV, ESV, NASB, RSV, NRSV) renders these as 'God' (capitalised when referring to YHWH) or 'god' (lowercase for false gods). The choice rejects that tradition in favour of the Hebrew root 'wl/'lh meaning 'to be strong/mighty'. NL pattern followed: NL renders all three as 'Machtige', explicitly stating in the master that 'God' is "een cultuurkeuze die de wortel niet bewaart" (a cultural choice that does not preserve the root). The same logic applies to English: 'God' is a tradition-loaded transliteration of a Germanic root unrelated to the Hebrew kernel. 'Mighty-One' preserves the root-meaning and works for both the Ene (singular interpretation of pluralis-majestatis) and for actual gods/judges (where the plural is genuine). Distribution analysis confirms this: across all 2,567 occurrences of elohim, the kernel is 'mighty-being-with-authority', not the abstract Western philosophical 'deity'.

H430 elohim is grammatically plural but predominantly semantically singular (pluralis-majestatis). The render-layer marks the plural-of-gods or plural-of-judges contexts. The concordant master keeps singular 'Mighty-One' as the citation form.

External-reference NRSV occasionally uses 'Almighty' which is semantically near 'Mighty-One' but reserves it for Shaddai. We do not follow NRSV here; 'Mighty-One' is the consistent English-root-rendering.

#### Emunah / Emet split (H530, H571)

**H530 emunah -> faithfulness**
**H571 emet -> truth-faithfulness**

NL renders both as 'trouw'. English does not have a single word covering both relational steadfastness and propositional truth — 'faithfulness' leans relational, 'truth' leans propositional. Decision: H530 emunah holds the relational kernel ('faithfulness'); H571 emet holds the dual-kernel via hyphenation ('truth-faithfulness') because English readers will encounter emet in propositional contexts (Ps 119:142 'Your law is truth') where 'faithfulness' alone would feel inadequate. This is a small departure from NL's collapsing both to 'trouw' — justified by English's lexical separation of these senses. Each Strong-code retains its own consistent rendering across all occurrences.

#### Adon and adam-distinction reaffirmed

**H113 adon (lord)** vs **H120 adam (human)** vs **H376 ish (man)**: Three distinct lexemes, three distinct English renderings. KJV often blurs them (using 'man' for both adam and ish). The seven-anchor analysis: ish is the gendered male individual, adam is the species-noun (gender-neutral), adon is the authority-relation. English readers will see 'human' for adam and 'man' for ish, which is concordantly correct. Confirmed in batch 4 with H376 fully assigned.

The Hebrew ayil cluster is one of the more complex polysemies in the corpus. Six related Strong-codes:
H352a 'ram' — male sheep
H352b 'doorpost-pillar' — architectural
H352c 'aristocrat-noble-leader' — figurative
H352d 'oaks-terebinths-mighty-trees' — botanical
H359a 'Elath' — port-city
H359b 'Eloth' — port-city variant

KJV uses 'ram/post/mighty/oak' depending on context, blurring the lexical-vs-homonym distinction. We preserve the master's six-fold split, with each Strong-code rendered concordantly. The figurative 'aristocrat-noble-leader' (H352c) is hyphenated to mark that this is not 'ram in metaphorical use' but a distinct lexical entry.

#### Aman-family verbs and derivatives (H539, H540, H543, H544, H551, H552)

**H539 aman -> believe**
The verb-of-trust. NL 'geloven'. KJV uses 'believe/trust'. The Hiphil-stem (predominant) means 'cause-to-stand-firm-on', and the kernel is the relational stand-firm-on relationship. 'Believe' (preserving the trust-dimension) is concordant; 'trust' is sometimes contextually appropriate but 'believe' is the lexical kernel.

**H543 amen -> amen**
Standard transliteration. Liturgical-affirmation form preserved.

**H394 akzari -> cruel-merciless**

Two related Strong-codes both rendering 'cruel-merciless' but with different morphology. H393 is the substantive-adjective; H394 is the gentilic-adjective form. Distinguished by hyphen-extension where H393 carries 'hard-fierce' as additional aspects.

### Batch 12 (H2257-H2490b, 2026-04-29) — 250 entries

#### Chaval-family rope-and-pledge cluster (H2256a-H2260, H2254a-c)

Batch 12 opens within the chaval root-complex, which holds three distinct semantic poles unified by the kernel 'twist/bind': rope-objects (chevel cord, chovel sailor, chibbel mast), pledge-objects (chavol pledge-pawn, chavolah borrowed-pledge), and the verb-of-binding-in-security itself. The seven-anchor distribution analysis confirms that what unifies sailor, pledge-taker, and rope is the act of binding-with-twisted-cord. KJV splits these renderings inconsistently across 'sailor/pilot/cord/destroy/pledge/band'. The concordant minimum holds: each Strong-code carries a hyphenated rendering whose first member preserves the binding-kernel and whose second member specifies the object-of-binding. H2259 chovel 'sailor-pilot' explicitly preserves rope-handling; H2260 chibbel 'ship-mast-rigging' marks the cord-and-pole-complex.

#### Chevel-flower naming (H2261)

**H2261 chavatstseleth -> meadow-saffron-crocus**
KJV/ESV/NIV use 'rose' (Song 2:1; Isa 35:1). This is botanically incorrect: the rose was unknown in Iron-Age Palestine and 'rose' is a Latin loan that does not match the Hebrew morphology (chavatstseleth derives from a bulb-flower root). The seven-anchor analysis points to a bulb-flowering plant, most plausibly a meadow-saffron, narcissus, or crocus. NL pattern followed (veldlelie-narcis-krokus). The hyphenated rendering preserves all three botanically-defensible candidates without committing to a single botanical species the source itself does not specify.

#### Chazah-vision-family (H2370, H2372, H2374, H2376-H2380, H2384)

The chazah-cluster is one of the most lexically dense in batch 12 due to its theological and prophetic centrality. Six related vision-words across Hebrew and Aramaic:
H2370 chazah-Aram 'behold-aramaic'
H2372 chazah 'behold-perceive'
H2374 chozeh 'seer'
H2376 chezu-aramaic 'vision-aramaic'
H2377 chazon 'vision' (the prophetic-revelation kernel)
H2378 chazot 'vision-record'
H2379 chazot-aramaic 'visibility-aramaic'
H2380 chazut 'conspicuous-vision'
H2384 chizzayon 'vision-revelation' (intensified-form)

KJV uses 'vision/seer/look/behold' near-interchangeably. We preserve the master's split with each Strong-code carrying its own English rendering. The kernel chazah is 'behold-perceive' (more receptive than ra'ah 'see' which can be active observation); the derived nouns split by morphology into prophet-receiving-vision (chazon), the vision-as-revelation-event (chizzayon), and the recorded-vision-document (chazot). External-reference: NIV uses 'vision' for chazon and chizzayon interchangeably; we keep them lexically distinct via 'vision' vs 'vision-revelation'.

#### Chazaq-strength-cluster (H2388-H2394)

**H2388 chazaq -> be-strong-take-hold**
The verb covers two distinct but related kernels: 'be-firm/strong internally' (Qal-stative) and 'lay-hold-of/grasp externally' (Hi/Pi-active). KJV splits these depending on context; we preserve both via the hyphenated rendering, which mirrors the NL approach (vast-zijn includes both senses). Distribution analysis across all 290 occurrences confirms the dual-aspect kernel: the same verb describes Pharaoh's heart being firm-internally and YHWH's hand laying-hold-externally.

**H2389 chazaq -> strong** (adjective, 56 occurrences)
**H2390 chazeq -> firm-stiff** (variant adjective, marks rigidity-aspect)
**H2392 chozeq -> firmness-power** (gripping-strength noun)
**H2393 chezqah -> strength-firmness** (stable-state noun)
**H2394 chozqah -> strong-grip** (coercive-grasping noun)

The morphological cluster preserves the Hebrew lexical distinctions in English. KJV collapses many of these to 'strength/might/force'; the concordant choice keeps each Strong-code distinct via hyphenated compound renderings whose components reflect the morphological-and-semantic gradient.

#### Chata-sin-family (H2398-H2403b)

**H2398 chata -> miss-mark-sin**
This is one of the more consequential rationale-decisions in batch 12. KJV/ESV/NIV use 'sin' alone, which has accumulated theological weight that exceeds the Hebrew kernel. The seven-anchor analysis: chata is fundamentally 'to miss the mark' (cf Judg 20:16 of slingers who 'do not chata' — i.e. do not miss). The moral-religious sense of 'sin' is a derivative use, not the kernel. We preserve both via the hyphenated 'miss-mark-sin'. External-reference: NRSV occasionally uses 'go astray' or 'err' for the non-cultic uses; we keep the lexical-kernel preserved across all 238 occurrences.

**H2403a chattaah -> sin** vs **H2403b chattat -> sin-offering**
Hebrew uses chattaah/chattat for both the abstract failing and the cultic offering for it. The English rendering must preserve the distinction because the offering and the act-of-failure are categorially different. Concordant choice: H2403a holds the abstract sense ('sin'), H2403b holds the cultic sense ('sin-offering'). NL pattern followed.

#### Chayah-life-family (H2416a-e, H2421, H2422, H2424, H2425)

**H2416a chay -> alive-living**
